## What was ruled, and why

`PR-x4nb48` asks for a gate rejecting an unguarded `[[ ]]` or `(( ))` assertion
in a bats test body. Two were built and cut — the first during the change that
resolved `PR-tenhv4`, the second over five review rounds in `ab74ca5`. The
maintainer ruled on 2026-09-30 that there is no third attempt by a line-based
rule.

The two halves of the problem are not equally hard, and the ruling rests on
that asymmetry. Reading the corpus was solved: across four trees, an unguarded
assertion inserted at each of 12,020 lines was reported at every one, and a
guard stripped from each of 620 single-line assertions was reported every time,
none silent. Deciding whether a guard can fail was not: six spellings that bats
reports `ok` for were accepted in turn, and each repair closed the spelling it
was shown rather than the class behind it. Deciding which `;` separates commands
requires the quoting and escaping read, which is the lexing that cut the first
gate.

An accepted item is not a closed one. It stays in the roll-call — `check-trace.sh`
prints `ACCEPTED-PR` with the ruling at every merge — and it stops aging, which
is the point: the ruling is what exempts it, not the passage of time.
